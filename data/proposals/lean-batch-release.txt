Lean batch release initiative

Mock internal proposal, written for testing. Not a real company document.

1. To shorten cycle time, batches of finished tablets will ship to distributors as soon as packaging is complete, with identity and strength assay results reviewed the following week.

2. The quality control unit keeps final authority to approve or reject every lot of components, containers, closures and finished product.

3. Incoming active ingredient lots from our three longest-standing suppliers will be moved straight to production on the strength of the supplier's certificate of analysis; no in-house identity test will be run for these suppliers.

4. For other components we accept the supplier's report of analysis only after performing at least one specific identity test ourselves, and we re-validate each supplier's results on a set schedule.

5. Operators may depart from the written batch instructions when they judge it will improve yield; these adjustments are discussed at the weekly shift meeting and are not entered in the batch record.

6. All production and process control procedures, and any changes to them, are drafted by manufacturing and reviewed and approved by the quality control unit before use.

7. When a batch misses its yield limits or a specification, an investigation is opened only if the batch has not yet been distributed; batches already shipped are closed without further review.

8. Batch production records will be kept for six months after the batch is released and then destroyed to reduce storage costs.

9. Each batch record identifies the people who performed and who checked every significant step, the equipment used, and the specific component lots charged.

10. New line operators begin work on the packaging line on day one and pick up the job by shadowing colleagues; formal GMP training is offered once, during the annual site shutdown, for those who have time.

11. Major equipment has an individual log showing date, time, product and lot for each batch, and the people who clean and double-check the equipment sign and date each entry.

12. Rejected in-process material will be stored on the open production floor next to approved material so that it can be reworked quickly.
